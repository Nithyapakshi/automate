Q roadmap

Done

  • Q's brain live on its own private server
  • Secure API: every request needs a private key, over HTTPS
  • Always on, reachable from anywhere

Now

  • Tap-to-talk Q app on the phone home screen

  • Text box that works with Wispr

Next

AI Builders

Useful builds and ideas from people building in public with AI.

Newest first. Starting with James Raybould; more builders will follow. Dates marked ~ are approximate, based on LinkedIn's relative ages checked 5 Oct 2026.

~5 Oct 2026 · James Raybould / LinkedIn

Start with the easiest repeated chore

Sharing Scott Belsky's piece, James suggests asking the assistant itself where to start. Email triage or basic research are easy first uses; don't overthink the entry point.

Why it matters: pick one repeated chore before designing the whole assistant.

Read the post on LinkedIn

~4 Oct 2026 · James Raybould / comment

From text digests to voice-to-voice

His assistant now talks back. He tests and shapes the experience live, filing bugs and ideas mid-conversation for a builder agent. He has not yet seen a fix completed during the same conversation. He reports around 3-second latency and switches between Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek.

Why it matters: voice-first assistants let people shape the experience while using it.

Source: James's reply to Doug Myers under his own LinkedIn post. Screenshot matched to the signed-in thread; link opens the parent post, not the exact reply.

Open the post and comments

~4 Oct 2026 · James Raybould / LinkedIn

A team of assistants, not one tool

James reports 4,952 texts with Instinct, 1,000+ with his own Q and 500+ with Muse over a month. The assistants know about each other, and he says some of the best work happens when several are involved.

Why it matters: give each assistant a clear role and let them work together.

Self-reported activity, not a productivity benchmark. He explicitly says feeling productive is not the same as being productive.

Read the post on LinkedIn

~3 Oct 2026 · James Raybould / LinkedIn

Apps become plumbing

He expects to visit 10-12 apps instead of 30 while still using the services behind all 30. Assistants would make more of the trips through each app's front door for him.

Why it matters: build assistants around tool access, not more screens.

Read the post on LinkedIn

~2 Oct 2026 · James Raybould / LinkedIn

Voice mode with interruption and no dead air

James says Q spans his messages, email, Slack, calendar, tasks, notes, transcripts and workout data. He reports 55 tools and 66,000 lines of code he did not write or review. His voice v1 works over car Bluetooth, can be interrupted mid-answer and says "one sec" when slow.

Why it matters: interruption and a short filler phrase are concrete features to consider for a voice v1.

His own report; voice mode is not yet fully ready for prime time.

Read the post on LinkedIn

23 Sep 2026 · James Raybould / LinkedIn

Keeping AI assistants swappable

He keeps code in GitHub, hosting on Vercel, data in Turso and Supabase, notes in Notion and email/calendar/docs in Google. Any AI assistant can connect to those same layers without owning them.

Why it matters: keep assistant tools and data independent of the model.

Read the post on LinkedIn

20 Sep 2026 · James Raybould / LinkedIn

The staff of a minor royal

Instinct, Muse and his own Q, built with Claude Code, watch things he never had time to track: camp signups, books, music, local updates and race results.

Why it matters: give each watch a clear signal so useful changes arrive without another manual check.
